DAT / 02 Drop photometric stereo and you lose geometry

One dark image,
turned in three dimensions.

Every surface below is reconstructed from a single GlowTact tactile frame — the darkening alone, with no second view and no marker field. Each object turns through a full revolution so the relief can be read from every angle.

GlowTact was not designed to recover shape — it was designed to visualise pressure. The relief below comes from running 9DTact’s reconstruction pipeline on GlowTact frames, and it resolves M1 threads at 0.25 mm pitch, where 9DTact’s own published results stop at M4 — 0.7 mm.

TURNTABLE

Every panel is GlowTact output. Relief is recovered from pressure-induced darkening in one tactile frame and is not validated against measured heights, so each surface should be read as spatial structure rather than exact geometry. The small frame on each card is the tactile image the reconstruction came from. The 9DTact figures are qualitative context: implementations and object sets differ, and no controlled quantitative comparison is claimed.

DAT / 03 One scalar image cannot give you direction

It reads the sideways pull.

A dense tangential field recovered from the tactile image alone — no markers printed in the gel, nothing tracked but the speckle already in it. Dense Farnebäck flow on a 26 px grid, gated by texture confidence and neighbour coherence, yields a usable field over 97.8 % of the gel (72.0 % under plain CLAHE), with a forward–backward consistency of 0.037 px median.

Arrow direction and colour give the local displacement; the red outline is the contact region. The strip along the bottom reports four readouts per frame: slide (mean tangential shift, px), twist (rotation ×10³), spread (push-out ×10³, the signature of a normal press) and slip (the residual after one affine fit; it rises when part of the patch breaks away). The backdrop is denoised and faded for legibility; the field is computed on the untouched frame.

DAT / 04 Darkening is qualitative; you cannot get newtons

Darkness carries the load.

Across four everyday objects, a learned regressor reads normal force from a GlowTact frame to 0.106 N mean absolute error, against 0.145 N for GelSight Mini — identical architecture, identical protocol, held-out contact locations.

Identical ResNet-18 regressors, ImageNet-pretrained, trained independently per sensor on 14716 frames — 13116 from ten probe geometries and 1600 from the four objects. Ninety per cent of spatial locations train, ten validate, with every depth from one indentation kept on the same side of the split so no loading trajectory leaks across it.

Force is estimated by a learned model from the image, not measured by the camera. The gain is concentrated below 2 N, which is where the optical response is strongest and where the sensor is designed to work; above 2 N GelSight Mini is the more accurate of the two. Overall RMSE across the controlled probes is 0.276 N against 0.285 N.

DAT / 05 A nitrile membrane must be fragile

The skin takes a blade.

The sensing surface is a sheet of black nitrile glove stretched over the gel — unbonded, and replaceable in minutes. Two clips put a knife to it.

These are qualitative demonstrations. There is no protocol, no cycle count and no lifetime test behind them, and nothing here supports a durability margin or a claim that the sensor is more robust than any other. They exist because the paper’s conclusion claims a more durable skin, and this is where that claim has to be answered.
